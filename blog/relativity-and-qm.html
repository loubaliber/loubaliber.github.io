On the Incompatibility of Theory of Relativity and Quantum Mechanics

The study of nature and the phenomena called “Physics” was an extensive, rigorous and optimistic attempt of understanding the intricacies of our origin. Long before the emergence of Physics, ancient philosophers were among the pioneers of science and are first to propose conjectural postulations governing nature. Before Newton unified the laws of motion, Galileo Galilei’s observations gave rise to the basis of mechanics and dynamics of heavenly bodies which further prompted the beginning of the theory of relativity expanded by Albert Einstein. After a century of innovation and research, a myriad of theories emerged attempting to unify the four fundamental forces in physics — gravity, electromagnetism, weak force, and the strong force. This complete unification of the forces also known as “The Theory of Everything” challenged physicist up until this very day. But, how do these forces really coexist?

Before the 1860s, electricity and magnetism were thought to be separate forces. Yet, James Clerk Maxwell thought otherwise and formulated the Maxwell’s equations. This led the distinction between electricity and magnetism coalesce into electromagnetism and predicted that electromagnetic waves travel at the speed $c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon_0 \mu_0}}$. However, at that time, physicist had a notion that most waves travel in a medium. If the speed of sound is relative to air, and the speed of the ocean wave is relative to water, then what is the speed of light relative to?

In the early 1900s, physicist propose a stationary reference frame that light travels through called, “aether”, which was later debunked by the Michelson-Morley experiment. This elicited Einstein to develop general relativity, which shows that gravity is actually the curvature of spacetime caused by mass and energy. Meanwhile, Max Planck have already solved one of the problems in classical physics, the blackbody radiation, stating that a hot object should emit infinite energy at small wavelengths. He introduced the quantization of energy leading to the birth of quantum mechanics. Einstein — being the genius that he is — tried to unify gravity with electromagnetism, an endeavor that was cut short due to the emergence of quantum mechanics. This was the start of quantum field theory (QFT). Paul Dirac extended the non-relativsitc Schrodinger’s equation into a relativistic Dirac equation. This equation played a central role in predicting the existence of antimatter and led the groundwork for quantum electrodynamics (QED), the relativistic theory of light-matter interactions. QFT also generalized the principles of QED to other forces beyond electromagnetism: the strong and the weak forces. Physicist applied the ideas of QFT developing quantum chromodynamics (QCD) for the strong force and to try to unify electromagnetism with the weak force.

However, there is a problem. The weak nuclear force and electromagnetism initially seemed to be distinct and unrelated. The weak force causes radioactive decay while electromagnetism governs electromagnetic interactions. During the 1960s, the Weinberg-Salam-Glashow Theory is introduced which states that the weak force and electromagnetism unify into a single force at high energies called the “electroweak force”. Still and all, this idea remained difficult to explain until Peter Higgs proposed the Higgs mechanism. He imagined the universe to be filled with a field called the Higgs field. Particles responsible for the weak forces slows down while moving through this field, gaining mass. Contrarily, particles such as photons, which is responsible for electromagnetism, doesn’t interact with the Higgs field and remain massless. In spite of the thought put into this theory, experimental confirmations are still needed. This was later confirmed by the discovery of W and Z bosons. Moreover, at even higher energies, the strong force is predicted to unify with the electroweak force into a Grand Unified Theory (GUT), something that is not yet confirmed by experiments. Nevertheless, the four fundamental forces are still far from being unified. Gravity is still isolated from the three other forces. General relativity treats gravity as a curvature of spacetime while quantum mechanics describes forces as an exchange of force-carrying particles. While we can attempt to quantize gravity, it leads to non-renormalizable infinities, making it insanely difficult to resolve.

Although our current understanding of physics steers us on the incompatibility of theory of relativity and quantum mechanics in addition to the current state of the discoveries in physics being relatively slower in the past decades, our ability to share information have been more convenient as ever. As artificial intelligence and machine learning models gain traction, newer techniques in obtaining comprehension about our universe is within our fingertips. Despite the gap of knowledge hindering our progress in developing a Theory of Everything — having our backs against the wall, perhaps we can try to look back once in a while on how things came about, appreciate what we accomplished, and gain inspiration on what more we can achieve.
