Research

Soft matter, seen through a fluorescence microscope.

Current work

Gelation of FITC-labeled kappa/lambda carrageenan.

My MSc thesis characterizes the gelation mechanism and network formation of FITC-labeled mixed kappa/lambda carrageenan gels. As an exchange researcher in the Physical Chemistry group at Tokyo University of Marine Science and Technology, I'm extending this into phase-separated biopolymer networks — correlating structural heterogeneity in the gel with the dynamics of embedded tracer particles.

Earlier work characterized fibrin-carrageenan mixtures as a hydrogel model for skeletal-muscle tissue engineering, using passive particle tracking alongside atomic force microscopy (AFM).

Methods

From the bench to the dataset.

01

Passive particle tracking & microrheology

Tracking the Brownian motion of embedded tracer beads via video microscopy to extract mean-squared displacement (MSD) and local viscoelastic moduli, reproducible within ±3% measurement error.

02

Fluorescence microscopy & image analysis

Custom Mathematica and Python/MATLAB pipelines process thousands of microscopy frames to quantify network structure and particle dynamics.

03

Optical tweezers

Micromanipulation and force measurement on complex fluids to quantify local viscoelastic properties of biological samples.

04

Statistical modeling & fractal analysis

Characterizing gel-network topology and surface roughness through fractal-dimension analysis and statistical modeling of phase-separated systems.
